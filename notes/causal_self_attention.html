What is hs?

hs is the length of one head's q, k or v vector for one token. c_attn gives every token a full query of C = 768 numbers. That vector is cut into nh = 12 consecutive slices of 64 numbers, and head h only ever uses slice h. The same cut is applied to k and v.

channel 0one token's q · 768 numbers767
nh × hs = C

12 × 64 = 768. More heads means shorter vectors per head; the total width and compute stay the same.

score = q·k over hs numbers

Inside one head, an attention score is the dot product of two 64-number vectors: a query slice and a key slice.

÷ √hs = ÷ 8

That is 1/math.sqrt(k.size(-1)). It keeps scores from growing with vector length, so softmax doesn't saturate.

No per-head weights

There's one C × 3C weight. "Heads" are just which output columns you read; view draws the boundaries.

A single (1024 × 64) matmul uses a small fraction of a GPU's cores, and each launch has fixed overhead. Batching the heads turns many small jobs into a few large ones. In Section 2, F.scaled_dot_product_attention replaces the score, mask, softmax and weighted-sum lines with one fused kernel that never stores the T × T matrix.
